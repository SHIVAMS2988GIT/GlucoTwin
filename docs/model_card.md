# Model Card: GlucoTwin Synthetic Glucose-Spike Risk

## Intended use
Demonstrate an end-to-end software pattern for combining historical EHR-like data and wearable-style time series. Intended for hackathon evaluation, learning, and interface demonstration only.

## Prohibited use
- Diagnosis, triage, medication or insulin dosing, or treatment decisions.
- Real-world patient care or claims of clinical efficacy.
- Use as a substitute for a clinician or validated continuous glucose monitoring system.

## Model
- Algorithm: scikit-learn RandomForestClassifier.
- Features: age, diabetes duration, HbA1c, adherence proxy, hypertension, current simulated glucose, 30-minute glucose change, heart rate, HRV, sleep, steps, meal carbohydrate scenario, stress scenario.
- Output: probability of a synthetic target label, plus illustrative lower/moderate/elevated categories.
- Horizon: 2 hours, as represented by the synthetic training-generation process.

## Data and target
All bundled data are generated by `backend/scripts/generate_data.py` using NumPy random distributions and a fixed seed. The binary target is stochastically generated from a formula influenced by scenario carbohydrates, recent glucose change, HbA1c, stress, activity, and the simulated future glucose change over a two-hour window. It is not a directly measured event, a hard glucose threshold, or a clinically grounded outcome definition. The data are not observations of real people.

## Evaluation
The training script uses a random holdout split of synthetic generated examples and reports accuracy, precision, recall, F1 and ROC-AUC where possible. Because examples from the same synthetic patient can appear in both splits, these metrics may be optimistic and should be treated only as software sanity checks. A stronger research evaluation would split by patient, use temporal validation, compare baselines, calibrate probabilities, quantify uncertainty, and evaluate on an independently sourced, appropriately licensed dataset.

## Fairness and limitations
Synthetic distributions may not reflect real populations. Performance may vary across age, sex, ethnicity, comorbidity, treatment regimen, sensor type, and missingness patterns. No fairness or subgroup validation has been performed. The model may learn artifacts of the synthetic label-generation rule rather than meaningful physiology.

## Monitoring and validation needed before any clinical use
- Clinical expert review of endpoint and prediction horizon.
- Independent external and prospective validation.
- Calibration, subgroup performance, missing-data robustness, drift monitoring.
- Privacy/security assessment and regulatory review.
- Human factors testing and a clinician-in-the-loop workflow.
